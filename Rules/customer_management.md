# Customer Management Guide

In these projects, your assigned instructor acts as your customer.

Use this guide to run effective customer conversations and keep requirements aligned throughout the final week.

## Goals of Customer Management

- Understand the real problem before building too much.
- Validate priorities early and often.
- Reduce rework by confirming assumptions.
- Build trust through clear communication and follow-through.

## 1. Run the Initial Customer Meeting

Use the first meeting to clarify outcomes, scope, and priorities.

### Suggested Agenda (30-45 minutes)

1. Introductions and roles (team + customer).
2. Business/problem context.
3. Success criteria for the final week.
4. Core user flows and priority features.
5. Non-functional expectations (performance, reliability, usability).
6. Constraints, assumptions, and open questions.
7. Agree communication cadence and next meeting.

### Questions to Ask

- What are the top 2-3 outcomes you care about most?
- Which features are must-have vs nice-to-have?
- What does a successful demo look like to you?
- Which user journey should work first?
- Are there any rules, edge cases, or constraints we should not miss?

## 2. Manage Scope with Priorities

After the initial meeting, convert requirements into three buckets:

- Must Have: required for an acceptable final demo.
- Should Have: important but only after core flow is stable.
- Could Have: stretch goals if time allows.

Share this prioritization with the customer and ask for explicit confirmation.

## 3. Get Regular Feedback on UI and Features

Do not wait until the end to show progress.

### Feedback Cadence (Recommended)

- 2-3 short touchpoints per week (15-20 minutes).
- One focused UI review when wireframes or first screens are ready.
- One focused feature review for backend/API behavior.

### What to Demo in Feedback Sessions

- What was completed since last meeting.
- What is in progress now.
- Any decisions that need customer input.
- Any risks that could affect scope or timeline.

Use simple artifacts for discussion (screenshots, mockups, API examples, short live demos).

## 4. Arrange Follow-Up Meetings Effectively

Each meeting should end with a plan for the next one.

- Confirm date/time before ending the call.
- Share a clear objective for the next meeting.
- Send pre-reads (if needed) at least a few hours in advance.
- Keep meetings time-boxed and outcome-focused.

## 5. Keep Useful Meeting Notes

Assign one note taker per meeting.

Capture:

- Date, attendees, and meeting purpose.
- Key decisions made.
- Open questions.
- Action items (owner + due date).
- Scope changes or priority updates.

Store notes in a shared team location that everyone can access.

## 6. Send Meeting Minutes After Every Customer Meeting

Send minutes within 24 hours.

### Suggested Minutes Template

Subject: Project Customer Meeting Minutes - YYYY-MM-DD

1. Summary
- 2-4 bullets on what was discussed.

2. Decisions
- Decision 1
- Decision 2

3. Actions
- Action, Owner, Due Date

4. Open Questions
- Question 1
- Question 2

5. Next Meeting
- Date/Time
- Objective

Ask the customer to confirm that decisions and priorities are accurate.

## 7. Handle Requirement Changes Safely

When the customer changes a requirement:

1. Restate the change in writing.
2. Explain impact on scope/time.
3. Propose options (swap scope, defer lower priority items, or extend effort).
4. Confirm the final decision and update your task plan.

## 8. Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- Waiting too long to show work.
- Assuming requirements without confirmation.
- Taking verbal decisions but not documenting them.
- Trying to build all features at once.
- Ignoring UI feedback until late in the week.

## 9. Quick Weekly Communication Rhythm (Example)

- Monday: short planning sync with customer (priorities for the week).
- Midweek: progress review (UI + feature feedback).
- End of week: demo + decisions for next steps.

Adjust cadence based on your customer's availability.
